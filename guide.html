DEFENSIVE REFERENCE

Audit what search engines can see.

A scoped operator reference and review workflow for domains you own or are explicitly authorized to test.

AUTHORIZATION REQUIREDUse these searches only for approved defensive audits. Do not seek, open, download, authenticate with, or retain other people's credentials, personal records, backups, or exposed infrastructure. Search visibility is not permission to access data.
// SAFE OPERATOR REFERENCE

Build a precise scope

site:example.com

Limit results to an approved domain.

filetype:pdf

Find a specific indexed document format. ext:pdf is a common alternative.

"exact phrase"

Match words together in the specified order.

-term

Exclude an irrelevant word from results.

intitle:term

Match text within an indexed page title.

inurl:term

Match text found within the indexed URL.

OR

Match either of two terms or conditions.

// DEFENSIVE AUDIT TEMPLATES

Start with your own domain

Replace example.com only with a domain covered by your written authorization. These templates locate indexed references; they do not authorize opening sensitive results.

site:example.com (filetype:pdf OR filetype:docx OR filetype:xlsx)
site:example.com ("internal use only" OR confidential OR restricted)
site:example.com (filetype:bak OR filetype:old OR filetype:zip)
site:example.com (intitle:"index of" OR intitle:"directory listing")
site:example.com (inurl:backup OR inurl:archive OR inurl:uploads)
site:example.com (password OR secret OR token OR api_key) -documentation -example -sample -tutorial

The final template is for spotting possible references to secrets in your own indexed content. Never place real credentials, customer data, account identifiers, or secret values into a search query.

// SAFER WORKFLOW

Review, fix, remove

  1. Create a written scope listing approved domains, subdomains, file stores, and testing dates.
  2. Begin with your organization's brand terms and known sensitive labels.
  3. Review titles, URLs, and harmless snippets where possible. Do not download files, bypass controls, or test discovered credentials.
  4. Record the URL, exposure type, and minimal evidence without copying sensitive content.
  5. Remove public access at the origin. Rotate possibly exposed secrets and invalidate affected sessions.
  6. After fixing the source, use Google Search Console's removal tools to accelerate de-indexing.
FIX THE SOURCE FIRSTSearch-result removal is not a substitute for access control. Protect confidential files at the origin, remove public links, and require authentication before requesting de-indexing.
